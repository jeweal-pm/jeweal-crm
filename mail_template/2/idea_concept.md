# GIS247 Inventory Visibility Journey

## Funnel purpose

Help jewelry retailers move from uncertain stock information to a practical conversation about real-time inventory, RFID, traceability, display zones, and replenishment.

## Audience and trigger

- Audience: jewelry retailers and store teams who struggle with stock counts, item location, transfers, or replenishment decisions.
- Trigger: a new GIS enquiry where inventory, stock, operations, or store control is relevant.
- Journey length: 6 steps across 8 days.
- Primary conversion: reply with the current inventory bottleneck or request a focused product review.

## Content journey

| Step | Timing | Marketing job | Topic | CTA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Immediately | Reassure and qualify | Inventory priority | Tell us the stock challenge |
| 2 | Day 1 | Educate | Real-time cloud inventory | See inventory visibility |
| 3 | Day 2 | Demonstrate speed | RFID stock counting | Explore RFID |
| 4 | Day 4 | Build confidence | Traceability and Zone | Follow an item's journey |
| 5 | Day 6 | Show action | Replenishment decisions | Explore replenishment |
| 6 | Day 8 | Convert respectfully | Focused review | Plan an inventory review |

## Stop and branch rules

- Stop when the contact replies, requests a quotation or demo, becomes an active opportunity, unsubscribes, or withdraws consent.
- If the contact replies about counting, prioritize RFID.
- If the contact replies about missing items or transfers, prioritize Traceability and Zone.
- If the contact replies about overstock or stockouts, prioritize Replenishment.

## Messaging principles

Use operational language, one problem per email, and avoid promising a specific result without discovery. Every email offers a reply path as well as a tracked CTA. The final step asks permission rather than forcing another message.
